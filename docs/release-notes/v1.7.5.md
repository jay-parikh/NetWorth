# ETFs, where they belong — and taxed the way they really are

Two things about ETFs in this release: they're now easy to add, and — more
importantly — the tax view finally treats them honestly.

**Every ETF is in the Scrip dropdown.** ETFs belong on the Equity tab: that's
how you buy them (through your broker) and how you hold them (in your demat
account). Until now you had to know an ETF's ISIN and type it in by hand.
Now every ETF is in the list, ready to pick — and that works even on a day
an exchange doesn't answer us.

**An ETF's value is never days old.** If no exchange quotes your ETF on a
given day, its fund NAV stands in — that's within a whisker of the traded
price, and far better than showing you last week's number. A real market
price always wins when there is one.

**The important one: gold, silver and bond ETFs are no longer taxed as
shares.** There's a new **Tax type** column on the Equity tab:

- Leave it **blank** for shares and Nifty/Sensex ETFs — nothing changes for you.
- Choose **Gold-Silver** for a gold, silver or overseas ETF.
- Choose **Debt** for a bond ETF.

Why it matters: shares and equity funds get a ₹1.25 lakh tax-free slice of
long-term gains each year. Gold, silver, overseas and bond ETFs **do not** —
they're taxed differently (gold and silver ETFs: 12.5% on long-term gains
after one year, with short-term gains at your own slab rate). Before this
release, a silver ETF sitting on the Equity tab quietly used up that ₹1.25
lakh allowance as though it were a share, which made your tax picture look
better than it is. Now those gains are shown separately, in a column named
**Non-equity fund gains**, and the allowance is left for what it's meant
for.

If you hold a gold or silver ETF, it's worth opening the Equity tab and
setting **Tax type** on that row. Everything else keeps working exactly as
before — the column only affects the Capital Gains tab, never your values,
your net worth or your returns.

As always: the Capital Gains tab is there for planning, not for filing.
