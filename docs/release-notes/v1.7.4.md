# Names, ETFs and shares that aren't listed yet

Four things from user reports, all in one release.

**Shares that aren't listed yet now have a home.** Pre-IPO holdings,
employee shares, a private company — fill the row in as usual and type
what one share is worth today in the new **Price if unlisted** column at
the end of the Equity tab. The holding is valued, counted in your net
worth and included in your return, with no exchange involved. The day the
company lists, the market price takes over by itself: the exchange price
always wins, and yours is used only while there is none. Nothing to
delete, nothing to switch. If you know the company's ISIN, put it in the
ISIN column and the changeover happens on listing day without you.

**ETFs and other NSE-only shares get their price more reliably.** NSE
sometimes refuses a download, and until now a single refusal cost the
whole day's NSE data. Anything listed only on NSE — most ETFs, like
Nippon India Silver ETF (SILVERBEES) — then quietly kept the previous
price while your other holdings updated. The updater now warms up its
connection the way a browser does, retries, and falls back to NSE's older
archive if the new one won't answer.

**A merger or demerger you type in yourself can now name the new
company.** The Corporate_Actions tab has two new columns, **New name**
and **New symbol**. Before this, a hand-entered demerger had nothing to
call the new company, so the new row was labelled with its ISIN — and
that label stuck for good. Existing rows repair themselves: the next time
the exchange tells us the real name, the ISIN label is replaced.

**The Tata Motors demerger is built in.** Held Tata Motors before
14-10-2025? You get your Tata Motors Limited (commercial vehicles) shares
1:1, with your cost split the way the company told shareholders to split
it — 68.85% stays with Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles, 31.15% moves to
the commercial-vehicle company — and both rows keep your original buy
date, so the long-term tax clock keeps running. Nothing to type; just
update. (If you already added it by hand, delete those rows first so they
don't double up.)

Your workbook is untouched by the update itself — as always, it's backed
up before every run.
